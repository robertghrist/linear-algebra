WEEK 12: PROBABILITY & HIGH DIMENSION
LAEF 2E -- Chapter 12: Probability & High Dimension (Sections 12.1-12.6)
Application sections: The Market Mode; The Blessing of Dimensionality

NEW CHAPTER FOR THE SECOND EDITION -- no first-edition problems exist for this week.
Prerequisite probability is the multivariable-calculus kind: densities integrated over
reasonable domains. Nothing measure-theoretic is assumed or should be tested.

THE CHAPTER'S THESIS: probability is not a new subject bolted on at the end. Expectation is
a projection, standard deviation is a length, correlation is a cosine, and the identities of
elementary statistics are Pythagoras in disguise. Then: in high dimension, chance stops
behaving like caprice and starts behaving lawfully.

CORE CONCEPTS:

1. The Geometry of Expectation (12.1)
   - Random variables on N outcomes ARE vectors in R^N; a density is a vector of positive
     weights summing to one
   - The probability inner product <f,g>_rho = sum rho_i f_i g_i (Def 12.1); continuous case
     replaces the sum by an integral
   - The constant vector 1 has unit length -- this IS the total-probability axiom
   - Expectation is an inner product with 1, hence E(f) 1 is the orthogonal projection onto
     the line of constants
   - Linearity of expectation needs no independence hypothesis because projection is linear
   - Centering is projection onto 1^perp
   - Variance is a squared length; standard deviation is a distance to the constants,
     which is why it carries the units of f
   - Covariance is the inner product of residuals; correlation is its cosine
   - Variances add exactly when residuals are orthogonal -- Pythagoras again
   - Conditional expectation is an orthogonal projection onto a subspace of random variables
   - Markov and Chebyshev inequalities (Lemma 12.2)

2. The Simplex (12.2)
   - The probability simplex Delta^{N-1} (Def 12.3): the nonnegative orthant met with the
     hyperplane <rho, 1> = 1
   - Vertices are point masses; the relative interior is the strictly positive densities
   - Stochastic matrices preserve the simplex (Lemma 12.4) -- Week 9's dynamics, now geometric
   - Softmax (Def 12.5) as the smooth map onto the simplex that Chapter 13 requires
   - Softmax is shift-invariant and its output is a density, never a probability of truth

3. High Dimension & Near-Orthogonality (12.3)
   - Two random directions in R^n meet at nearly a right angle, with overwhelming probability
   - Random inner products: mean 0 and variance 1/n (Lemma 12.6)
   - Concentration: random lengths crowd onto a thin shell
   - The union bound (Lemma 12.7) as the tool for controlling many events at once
   - The probabilistic method: prove existence by showing failure has probability below one
   - Exponentially many nearly-orthogonal directions exist in R^n

4. Random Matrices & the Noise Floor (12.4)
   - Singular values of a pure-noise matrix do NOT scatter arbitrarily
   - Total spectral mass equals the Frobenius norm, concentrated near nd by Chebyshev
   - The Marchenko-Pastur bulk with aspect ratio gamma = d/n and edges (1 -+ sqrt(gamma))^2
   - A singular value above the upper edge is signal; a singular value inside the bulk is not
   - Caution on notation: sigma(f) is a standard deviation; subscripted sigma_i are singular values
   - This is what makes Week 11's scree plot readable against a standard instead of by instinct

5. Random Projection (12.5)
   - Random projection Phi in R^{k x n} with independent mean-zero Gaussian entries of
     variance 1/k (Def 12.8); the variance is calibration, not decoration
   - Random projections measure length in expectation, with concentration (Lemma 12.9)
   - JOHNSON-LINDENSTRAUSS (Thm 12.10): for any m points in R^n and any 0 < epsilon < 1,
     a random projection to R^k with k >= 36 ln(m) / epsilon^2 preserves every pairwise
     SQUARED distance to relative error epsilon, with probability at least 1 - 1/m
   - The target dimension depends on the NUMBER OF POINTS and the tolerance -- NOT on n
   - Proof shape: one-point concentration plus a union bound over the C(m,2) pairs
   - Database-friendly variants; why this is the enabling result for nearest-neighbor search,
     clustering, and retrieval at scale

6. The Randomized SVD (12.6)
   - One Gaussian probe A omega returns a mixture of left singular vectors, each speaking at
     its native volume sigma_i
   - Stack k + p probes to form the sketch Y = A Omega with oversampling p
   - Orthonormalize the sketch (QR, Week 5) to capture the dominant range
   - Randomized Range Finder (Thm 12.11): the sketch's range approximates the top-k range
   - Project A onto that range and factor the small matrix exactly
   - Why it matters: the full SVD costs O(nd min(n,d)) and only the top k triples are wanted

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (v2, 2026-09-10 -- conceptual: recognize, decide, distinguish, explain why):
- [W12.S01] Translate between statistical and geometric vocabulary: expectation / projection
  onto constants, variance / squared length, covariance / inner product, correlation / cosine,
  centering / projection onto 1^⊥
- [W12.S02] Recognize the probability inner product ⟨f,g⟩_ρ, why the constant vector has unit
  length (total probability), and why linearity of expectation needs no independence
- [W12.S03] Recognize conditional expectation as an orthogonal projection, and when variances
  add (orthogonal residuals — Pythagoras)
- [W12.S04] Recognize the simplex, decide whether a point lies in it, and know what softmax does
  (a density on the simplex; shift-invariant; not a probability of correctness) and that
  stochastic matrices preserve the simplex
- [W12.S05] Explain near-orthogonality in high dimension: random inner products have mean 0 and
  variance 1/n; lengths concentrate; exponentially many nearly-orthogonal directions
- [W12.S06] Know what the union bound and the probabilistic method do, and recognize an argument
  of the shape "one-point concentration plus a union bound over pairs"
- [W12.S07] Know that pure noise has a predictable spectrum (a bulk with an edge), so a singular
  value is signal only if it clears the noise floor — and that this is what makes a scree plot
  readable
- [W12.S08] State what Johnson–Lindenstrauss preserves (pairwise SQUARED distances to relative
  error ε) and what the target dimension depends on (number of points and ε — NOT the ambient
  dimension); recognize the random projection's calibration
- [W12.S09] Describe the randomized SVD in order (probe, sketch, orthonormalize, project,
  factor) and say why it is cheap and approximate

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Believing linearity of expectation requires independence
- Reporting the JL target dimension as depending on the ambient dimension n
- Stating JL as preserving distances rather than SQUARED distances
- Forgetting the union bound over pairs and quoting a per-pair failure probability
- Confusing standard deviation sigma(f) with a singular value sigma_i
- Reading a large leading eigenvalue as signal without comparing it to the noise floor
- Treating softmax output as a probability that the answer is correct
- Assuming a random projection matrix is orthogonal (its rows are only approximately so)
- Dropping the 1/k variance calibration and then wondering why lengths are wrong
- Claiming the randomized SVD is exact, or that oversampling p is optional in practice
