CORE CONCEPTS:

1. The First Order (7.1)
   - The scalar equation x' = a x and its solution x(t) = e^{at} x(0)
   - Initial value problems; growth, decay, and the sign of a
   - The whole chapter is an argument that the matrix case should look like this

2. Coupled First-Order Systems (7.2)
   - x' = A x in matrix form
   - The diagonal case solves coordinate by coordinate
   - Chemical reaction networks as the running example (Ex 7.1)
   - Strategy: find a basis in which the system decouples

3. Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors (7.3)
   - Definition A v = lambda v with v nonzero (Def 7.2)
   - Existence of a nonzero solution requires det(A - lambda I) = 0
   - The characteristic polynomial (Lemma 7.4)
   - Eigenvalue relations: trace = sum of eigenvalues, determinant = product (Lemma 7.5)
   - Eigenspaces as subspaces; eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent
   - Geometric reading: directions the map only stretches

4. Simple Diagonalization (7.4)
   - A = V D V^{-1} when an eigenbasis exists (Thm 7.6, Def 7.7)
   - Eigenbasis criterion (Lemma 7.8)
   - n distinct eigenvalues is SUFFICIENT, not necessary
   - Matrix powers: A^k = V D^k V^{-1} (Lemma 7.9)
   - Diagonalization is a change of basis (Week 4) with a purpose

5. Matrix Exponentials (7.5)
   - Definition by the power series (Def 7.10)
   - e^{A} e^{B} = e^{A+B} only when A and B commute (Lemma 7.11)
   - x(t) = e^{tA} x(0) solves the initial value problem (Lemma 7.12)
   - Diagonalizable case: e^{tA} = V e^{tD} V^{-1} (Lemma 7.13)
   - Mass-spring systems (Ex 7.14)

6. Higher-Order Equations & Basis Solutions (7.6)
   - Reduction of an n-th order scalar ODE to a first-order system
   - The companion matrix and its characteristic polynomial
   - Basis solutions: distinct roots give e^{lambda_i t} (Thm 7.15)
   - The solution space of a linear ODE has dimension equal to the order (Week 2, Ex 2.7)
   - Matching initial conditions is a linear system, Vandermonde in the distinct roots

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (core; expected without hesitation):
- Compute eigenvalues and eigenvectors for 2x2 and 3x3 matrices, including triangular ones
- Use trace and determinant to check or to shortcut an eigenvalue computation
- Decide diagonalizability from eigenvector counts, not from distinctness alone
- Assemble V and D and verify A = V D V^{-1}
- Compute A^k and e^{tA} for a diagonalizable matrix
- Solve x' = Ax with a given initial condition and describe the long-run behavior from the spectrum
- Convert a higher-order scalar ODE to a first-order system and back
- Write a basis of solutions from distinct characteristic roots

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Concluding non-diagonalizability from a repeated eigenvalue alone
- Scaling an eigenvector and treating it as a different eigenvector
- Writing A = V^{-1} D V instead of V D V^{-1}
- Computing e^{A} entrywise as the exponential of each entry
- Using e^{A+B} = e^A e^B without checking that A and B commute
- Reading stability off the entries of A rather than off the eigenvalues
- Forgetting that a real matrix can have complex eigenvalues (Week 8 territory, but it appears here)
