CORE CONCEPTS:

1. The Spectral Theorem (10.1)
   - A real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and an orthonormal eigenbasis:
     A = Q D Q^T with Q orthogonal (Thm 10.1)
   - The spectral decomposition as a sum of rank-one projections, A = sum lambda_i q_i q_i^T
   - Positive semidefiniteness and the eigenvalue sign pattern
   - A^T A and A A^T are symmetric positive semidefinite with the same nonzero eigenvalues

2. Spheres, Ellipsoids, & Singular Values (10.2)
   - Every linear map sends the unit sphere to an ellipsoid (or a degenerate one)
   - Principal semi-axes are the singular values; their directions are the left singular vectors
   - Singular values as the square roots of the eigenvalues of A^T A (Def 10.2)
   - Two frames, not one: the input basis and the output basis are DIFFERENT (Ex 10.3)
     -- this is the essential contrast with diagonalization

3. Constructing the SVD (10.3)
   - Step 1: orthonormal eigenvectors of A^T A give V and the singular values sigma_i
   - Step 2: u_i = A v_i / sigma_i for sigma_i > 0 gives the left singular vectors
   - Step 3: complete U to an orthonormal basis using the cokernel
   - Existence for every matrix, square or not (Thm 10.4)
   - Uniqueness: singular values always; singular vectors up to sign and up to rotation
     within a repeated singular value's subspace

4. Interpreting the SVD (10.4)
   - A = U Sigma V^T = sum sigma_i u_i v_i^T: a sum of rank-one pieces in decreasing importance
   - Rank = number of nonzero singular values
   - Bases for all four fundamental subspaces read directly off U and V
     (v's beyond rank r span the kernel; u's beyond rank r span the cokernel)
   - The pseudoinverse in closed form: A^+ = V Sigma^+ U^T, with Sigma^+ inverting the
     nonzero singular values and transposing (connects to Week 6)
   - Ridge regression as singular-value filtering: sigma / (sigma^2 + lambda)

5. Invariance & Natural Structure (10.5)
   - Singular values are unchanged by A -> Q_1 A Q_2^T for orthogonal Q_1, Q_2
   - Matrix norms (Def 10.5): spectral norm = sigma_1; Frobenius norm^2 = sum sigma_i^2
   - Singular values under composition (Lemma 10.6)
   - Singular values as distances: sigma_{k+1} is the distance from A to the nearest
     rank-k matrix in the spectral norm (Lemma 10.7)
   - Condition number kappa(A) = sigma_1 / sigma_r (Def 10.8) -- Week 1's warning made exact
   - Near-singularity means a small singular value, not a small determinant

6. Inner Products & the SVD (10.6)
   - The construction transported to abstract inner product spaces via the adjoint T*
   - T*T and TT* play the roles of A^T A and A A^T
   - Finite-dimensional function spaces (Ex 10.9)
   - Text analysis (Ex 10.10); temperature sensor arrays (Ex 10.11)

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Reporting eigenvalues of A when singular values are asked for (they agree only for
  symmetric positive semidefinite A, up to absolute value for symmetric A)
- Expecting U = V for a non-symmetric matrix
- Forgetting that Sigma is rectangular and that Sigma^+ transposes as well as inverts
- Inverting a zero singular value when forming A^+
- Using the determinant as the measure of near-singularity instead of sigma_min
- Taking kappa = sigma_1 / sigma_n when the matrix is rank deficient (the denominator is sigma_r)
- Assuming singular values are similarity-invariant (they are ORTHOGONALLY invariant)
- Confusing the spectral norm with the Frobenius norm
