LAEF 2E -- Chapter 8: Eigenvalue Complexities (Sections 8.1-8.5)

CORE CONCEPTS:

1. Complex Eigenvalues & Oscillation (8.1)
   - Real matrices have complex eigenvalues in conjugate pairs, with conjugate eigenvectors (Ex 8.1)
   - Real normal form for a conjugate pair: the rotation-scaling 2x2 block (Lemma 8.2)
   - Euler's theorem redux: e^{(a+bi)t} as decaying/growing oscillation (Lemma 8.3)
   - Real solutions from complex data: take real and imaginary parts
   - a = Re(lambda) sets growth or decay; b = Im(lambda) sets frequency

2. Repeated Eigenvalues (8.2)
   - Algebraic vs. geometric multiplicity (Def 8.4)
   - Geometric <= algebraic, always; defectiveness is the gap
   - Generalized eigenvectors and generalized eigenspaces (Lemma 8.5, Ex 8.6)
   - Jordan chains: (A - lambda I) w = v
   - Exponentiating a simple block produces the t e^{lambda t} terms (Ex 8.7)

3. The Jordan Canonical Form (8.3)
   - Every matrix is similar to a Jordan form, unique up to block order (Thm 8.8)
   - Jordan blocks: one block per independent eigenvector; block sizes from the chain lengths
   - Reading structure from multiplicities (Ex 8.9, 8.10, 8.11)
   - What Jordan form buys: powers, exponentials, and the exact failure mode of diagonalization
   - Numerical caveat: the Jordan form is discontinuous in the entries and is not computed in practice

4. Computing Eigenvalues (8.4)
   - The characteristic polynomial is a proof device, not an algorithm
   - The QR algorithm: iterate A = QR, then A <- RQ (Def 8.12, Ex 8.13)
   - Similarity is preserved at every step, so the spectrum is preserved
   - Convergence to (quasi-)triangular form; shifts and practical considerations

5. Back to Basis (8.5)
   - Basis solutions for repeated roots: e^{lambda t}, t e^{lambda t}, ..., t^{m-1} e^{lambda t} (Thm 8.14)
   - Repeated real root (Ex 8.15); critical damping (Ex 8.16); repeated complex roots (Ex 8.17)
   - The polynomial factor is the ODE's signature of a defective matrix

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (core; expected without hesitation):
- Find complex eigenvalues and produce REAL solutions from them
- Read frequency and growth rate off a complex eigenvalue
- Compute algebraic and geometric multiplicity, and diagnose defectiveness
- Determine the Jordan block structure from multiplicities and ranks of powers of (A - lambda I)
- Find a generalized eigenvector by solving (A - lambda I) w = v
- Exponentiate a 2x2 or 3x3 Jordan block
- Write the basis of solutions for an ODE with repeated or complex characteristic roots
- Explain what one QR step does and why the spectrum is unchanged

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Reporting geometric multiplicity greater than algebraic
- Assuming a repeated eigenvalue forces a Jordan block (it need not; check the eigenspace)
- Counting Jordan blocks by algebraic rather than geometric multiplicity
- Omitting the t e^{lambda t} term for a defective repeated root
- Treating complex eigenvalues as evidence that a real system has complex behavior
  (the oscillation is real; only the bookkeeping is complex)
- Confusing the QR ALGORITHM (Week 8, eigenvalues) with the QR DECOMPOSITION (Week 5, factorization)
- Believing the Jordan form is what software computes
