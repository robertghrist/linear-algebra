WEEK 2: ABSTRACT VECTOR SPACES
LAEF 2E -- Chapter 2: Abstract Vector Spaces (Sections 2.1-2.5)
Application sections: Engineering Signals as Vector Spaces; Linear Differential Equations

NOTE ON SCOPE: Chapter 2 stops at "Towards Dimension." Bases, coordinates, and change of
basis are Chapter 4 (Week 4). Week 2 questions should test structure, not coordinates.

CORE CONCEPTS:

1. Vector Space Axioms (2.1)
   - Closure under addition and scalar multiplication
   - The eight axioms and what each one buys
   - Uniqueness of the zero vector and of additive inverses (Lemma 2.2)
   - Consequences that must be proved, not assumed (0v = 0, (-1)v = -v)

2. A Gallery of Vector Spaces (2.2)
   - Euclidean space R^n (Ex 2.3)
   - Matrix spaces (Ex 2.4)
   - Polynomial spaces, graded and ungraded (Ex 2.5)
   - Function spaces (Ex 2.6)
   - Solution sets of linear ODEs (Ex 2.7)
   - Sequences and series (Ex 2.8)
   - The point: the same theorems govern all of them

3. Subspaces (2.3)
   - Definition and the three-part test (Def 2.9)
   - Coordinate subspaces (Ex 2.10)
   - Null space (Ex 2.11) and column space (Ex 2.12) as subspaces attached to a matrix
   - Intersections and sums of subspaces; direct sums
   - Why the zero vector membership check catches most non-examples

4. Span & Linear Independence (2.4)
   - Span as the set of all linear combinations (Def 2.13)
   - Span of a set is always a subspace
   - Linear independence (Def 2.16) as a statement about the ONLY solution being trivial
   - Testing independence in R^n by row reduction (Ex 2.19)
   - Testing independence of polynomials and functions (Ex 2.18)

5. Towards Dimension (2.5)
   - Minimality and independence are the same condition (Lemma 2.20)
   - Minimal spanning sets (Lemma 2.21)
   - The Exchange Bound: an independent set never outnumbers a spanning set (Cor 2.22)
   - Dimension as the well-defined common size (Def 2.23)
   - Dimension of polynomial spaces (Ex 2.24) and infinite-dimensional function spaces (Ex 2.25)

FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES:
- Abstraction earns its keep by proving one theorem for many spaces at once
- Spanning is about coverage; independence is about non-redundancy; dimension is where they meet
- The Exchange Bound is the engine behind every dimension claim in the book

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (core; expected without hesitation):
- [W02.S01] Verify or refute the subspace property for a described set
- [W02.S02] Decide whether a given set spans a stated space
- [W02.S03] Test a finite set of vectors, matrices, or polynomials for linear independence
- [W02.S04] Compute the dimension of a described subspace (null space, column space, symmetric matrices,
  polynomials of bounded degree, solution sets of homogeneous systems)
- [W02.S05] Compare dim of a sum and an intersection of two subspaces
- [W02.S06] Recognize an infinite-dimensional space and say why it is one

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Accepting a set that is closed under addition but not scalar multiplication (or vice versa)
- Testing a nonhomogeneous solution set as a subspace (it is an affine set, not a subspace)
- Conflating "these vectors span R^3" with "these vectors are independent"
- Counting the number of generators rather than the dimension of their span
- Assuming a set of n vectors in an n-dimensional space is automatically a basis without a check
- Treating dim(U + W) = dim U + dim W when the intersection is nontrivial
