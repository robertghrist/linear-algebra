CORE CONCEPTS:

1. Covariance & Correlation (11.1)
   - Centering; covariance as an inner product of residuals
   - Covariance matrix and correlation matrix
   - Correlation as cosine similarity between centered variables
   - Symmetry and positive semidefiniteness of the covariance matrix

2. Matrices & Data (11.2)
   - The centered data matrix X: rows are observations, columns are variables
   - Covariance as C = (1/n) X^T X -- a Gram matrix
   - Data as a point cloud; the geometry of a cloud vs. the algebra of a matrix

3. Principal Components (11.3)
   - Principal components as eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, equivalently the right
     singular vectors of the centered data matrix (Def 11.1)
   - PC scores: coordinates of the data in the principal basis (Def 11.2)
   - Variance captured by each component equals the corresponding eigenvalue, equal to
     sigma_i^2 / n
   - Successive components: each maximizes remaining variance subject to orthogonality
   - Gene expression data (Ex 11.3)

4. Optimality Properties (11.4)
   - Principal Component Optimality (Thm 11.4): v_1 maximizes v^T C v over unit vectors, and
     each later v_k maximizes it subject to orthogonality with its predecessors
   - Section 11.4 then shows the equivalent readings: maximizing captured variance,
     minimizing mean squared reconstruction error, and minimizing distance shrinkage are
     the SAME problem, differing by constants
   - Caution: when an eigenvalue repeats, the maximizer is not unique
   - The Rayleigh quotient (Week 9) is the mechanism behind the maximization
   - Rank-k approximation (Def 11.5): truncate the SVD
   - ECKART-YOUNG-MIRSKY (Thm 11.6): the truncated SVD is the best rank-k approximation in
     both the spectral and the Frobenius norm, for k < r; the errors are exactly
     ||A - A_k||_2 = sigma_{k+1} and ||A - A_k||_F = sqrt(sum_{i>k} sigma_i^2)
   - Image compression as the visible instance (Ex 11.7)

5. Judgment (11.5)
   - Centering is mandatory; standardization is a decision
   - Covariance PCA vs. correlation PCA: units, dominance, and what each preserves (Ex 11.8)
   - Scree plots and explained-variance ratios; how many components to retain
   - Mahalanobis distance (Def 11.9): distance measured in units of the data's own spread
   - Outlier detection and why screening must PRECEDE the eigenvector computation (Ex 11.10)
   - Vibration analysis (Ex 11.11); chemical process monitoring (Ex 11.12)
   - Forward link: Section 12.4 supplies the noise floor against which a scree plot is read

6. Completion & Corruption (11.6)
   - Matrix completion problem (Def 11.13): recover a low-rank matrix from a subset of entries
   - Nuclear norm (Def 11.14) as the convex surrogate for rank -- the sum of singular values
   - Matrix completion guarantee (Thm 11.15) and its incoherence/sampling hypotheses
   - Robust PCA (Def 11.16): decompose M = L + S into low-rank plus sparse
   - Principal Component Pursuit (Thm 11.17): the hypothesis is on the SUPPORT of S being
     random, not on its signs
   - Video surveillance: background as L, moving objects as S (Ex 11.18)

7. Beyond Linear PCA (11.7)
   - When data lies near a curved manifold, linear projection misleads
   - Kernel PCA: replace the covariance matrix with a kernel matrix K = [k(x_i, x_j)]
     and run the same machinery in an implicit feature space
   - Autoencoders as the nonlinear successor (forward link to Week 13)

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (core; expected without hesitation):
- Center a small data matrix and compute its covariance matrix
- Identify principal components as eigenvectors of C or right singular vectors of X
- Compute variance captured and explained-variance ratios from eigenvalues or singular values
- Read a scree plot and defend a choice of k
- Decide between covariance PCA and correlation PCA from the units and scales given
- Produce the best rank-k approximation from a given SVD and state its error in both norms
- Compute a Mahalanobis distance and use it to flag an outlier
- Project data onto the top two components and interpret the resulting plot
- Interpret loadings: which variables drive which component, and the sign ambiguity
- State the nuclear norm and say why it stands in for rank

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Omitting the centering step and reporting the leading component as the mean direction
- Using covariance PCA on variables with wildly different units
- Confusing eigenvalues of C (variances) with singular values of X (their square roots, times sqrt(n))
- Reporting the Frobenius error when the spectral error is asked for, or vice versa
- Claiming the truncated SVD is merely a good approximation rather than the optimal one
- Treating a sign flip in a loading vector as a different component
- Interpreting a large first component as evidence of signal without a noise floor to compare against
- Assuming Robust PCA needs the sparse part to be small in magnitude (it needs it to be sparse)
- Writing "Eckart-Minsky-Young" (the theorem is Eckart-Young-Mirsky)
