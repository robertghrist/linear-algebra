CORE CONCEPTS:

1. At First Iteration (9.1)
   - x_{k+1} = A x_k; the solution x_k = A^k x_0
   - Eigen-decomposition of the initial condition; each mode scaled by lambda^k
   - Six-sector economy as the running example (Ex 9.1)
   - Contrast with Week 7: e^{lambda t} vs. lambda^k; Re(lambda) < 0 vs. |lambda| < 1

2. Dominance & Convergence (9.2)
   - Spectral radius rho(A) and dominant eigenvalue (Def 9.2)
   - A^k -> 0 exactly when rho(A) < 1
   - Dominant convergence: the iterate aligns with the dominant eigenvector (Lemma 9.3)
   - Rate of convergence set by the ratio |lambda_2| / |lambda_1|
   - The power method as this observation turned into an algorithm
   - Fibonacci redux (Ex 9.4); economic convergence (Ex 9.5)
   - Neumann series (Lemma 1.8) reappears for the equilibrium of an affine iteration

3. Positivity & Perron-Frobenius (9.3)
   - Perron-Frobenius for positive matrices: rho(A) is a simple eigenvalue with a positive
     eigenvector, strictly dominant (Thm 9.6)
   - Positivity conditions: nonnegative, irreducible, primitive, positive (Def 9.8) -- SEE THE TABLE
   - Frobenius extension to irreducible nonnegative matrices (Thm 9.9)
   - Irreducibility as strong connectivity of the associated digraph
   - Citation networks (Ex 9.7); catalytic cycles (Ex 9.10)

4. Stochastic Matrices & Markov Chains (9.4)
   - Markov chain, probability distribution, stochastic matrix (Defs 9.11, 9.12, 9.13)
   - Column-stochastic convention: columns sum to one, so A^T 1 = 1 and rho(A) = 1 (Lemma 9.15)
   - Stationary distribution as the eigenvector for lambda = 1
   - Markov convergence and its hypotheses (Thm 9.16)
   - Weather patterns (Ex 9.14, 9.17); random walk on a graph (Ex 9.18)

5. Symmetric Matrices & Spectra (9.5)
   - Symmetric matrices are orthogonally diagonalizable, with real eigenvalues (Lemma 9.19)
   - Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are automatically orthogonal
   - The Rayleigh quotient q(x) = x^T A x / x^T x; its extreme values are lambda_max and
     lambda_min, attained at the corresponding eigenvectors (Lemma 9.22)
   - Correlation and inertia (Ex 9.20); distance matrices (Ex 9.21)

6. Networked Behavior & Consensus (9.6)
   - Adjacency and degree; the Graph Laplacian L = D - A (Def 9.23)
   - L is symmetric positive semidefinite; the constant vector is in its kernel
   - dim ker L counts the connected components; the FIEDLER VECTOR, the eigenvector of the
     smallest nonzero eigenvalue, splits the graph into two groups
   - Redistribution and consensus: averaging dynamics converge to a common value (Thm 9.25)
   - Five cohorts (Ex 9.24); robotic flocking (Ex 9.26); supply chains (Ex 9.27)

  and contrast |λ| < 1 (iteration) with Re λ < 0 (ODE)
- [W09.S02] Identify the spectral radius and the dominant eigenvalue/eigenvector, and predict
  the limiting direction of an iterate and its rate (|λ₂|/|λ₁|)
- [W09.S03] Recognize the power method as dominant convergence turned into an algorithm — what
  it finds and when it fails
- [W09.S04] Classify a nonnegative matrix as irreducible and/or primitive, and know which
  Perron–Frobenius conclusion each hypothesis buys (simple dominant eigenvalue, positive
  eigenvector, strict dominance)
- [W09.S05] Recognize a (column-)stochastic matrix, know why λ = 1 is always an eigenvalue and ρ
  = 1, identify the stationary distribution, and know the hypotheses for convergence to it
- [W09.S06] Know what symmetry buys: real eigenvalues, orthogonal eigenvectors for DISTINCT
  eigenvalues, and an orthonormal eigenbasis that can be chosen (not automatic within a repeated
  eigenspace)
- [W09.S07] Recognize a Gram matrix XᵀX or a covariance matrix as symmetric positive
  semidefinite (positive definite iff independent columns), with eigenvalues ≥ 0
- [W09.S08] Know what the Rayleigh quotient measures and that its extremes are λ_max and λ_min
  at the eigenvectors
- [W09.S09] Recognize the Graph Laplacian, read the number of components off its kernel, and
  know what the Fiedler vector splits

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Using Re(lambda) < 0 (the ODE criterion) as the convergence test for an iteration
- Assuming every nonnegative matrix satisfies Perron-Frobenius (irreducibility is required)
- Confusing irreducible with primitive (a periodic chain is irreducible but not primitive)
- Mixing row-stochastic and column-stochastic conventions mid-problem
- Failing to normalize a stationary eigenvector to a probability distribution
- Asserting a symmetric matrix's eigenvectors are orthogonal when the eigenvalues coincide
  (they CAN be chosen orthogonal; they are not automatically so within an eigenspace)
- Treating rapid convergence of the Rayleigh quotient as convergence of the eigenvector
