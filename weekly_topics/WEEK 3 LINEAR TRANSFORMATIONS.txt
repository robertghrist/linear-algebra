WEEK 3: LINEAR TRANSFORMATIONS
LAEF 2E -- Chapter 3: Linear Transformations (Sections 3.1-3.8)
Application sections: Color & the Space of Light; Dimensional Analysis & the Drag on a Sphere;
Graph Topology & Network Structure

CORE CONCEPTS:

1. Euclidean Transformations (3.1)
   - Rotations, reflections, shears, projections, scalings in R^2 and R^3
   - Reading a matrix off the images of the standard basis vectors
   - Composition as matrix multiplication

2. Linear Transformations (3.2)
   - Definition: additivity and homogeneity (Def 3.1)
   - Immediate consequences: T(0) = 0, T preserves linear combinations (Lemmas 3.2, 3.3)
   - Domain, codomain, and why the codomain is a choice
   - Differentiation (Ex 3.4) and integration (Ex 3.5) as the running non-matrix examples

3. Isomorphisms (3.3)
   - Injective and surjective (Def 3.6, Lemma 3.7)
   - Isomorphism (Def 3.8): a linear bijection
   - Coordinate vectors as the first isomorphism a student meets (Ex 3.9)
   - Two spaces of the same finite dimension are isomorphic

4. The Four Fundamental Spaces
   - Image (Def 3.11) and Kernel (Def 3.12), defined for any linear map
   - Coimage (Def 3.22) and Cokernel (Def 3.23) as quotients
   - Where each one lives: kernel and coimage in the domain; image and cokernel in the codomain
   - Blake's Four Zoas correspondence (kernel/Luvah, coimage/Tharmas, image/Urizen,
     cokernel/Urthona) -- thematic, not examinable

5. Rank & Nullity (3.5)
   - Definitions as dimensions of image and kernel (Def 3.14)
   - Agreement with the pivot count of Chapter 1 (Ex 3.15)
   - Rank and nullity for calculus operators (Ex 3.16, 3.17)

6. Quotients (3.6)
   - Quotient space and cosets (Def 3.18)
   - Kernel quotients (Ex 3.19), geometric quotients (Ex 3.20)
   - Quotient as "the information that survives"; dim(V/U) = dim V - dim U

7. The Fundamental Theorem (3.8)
   - Statement: V = ker T (+) coimage T, W = im T (+) cokernel T, with coimage T isomorphic
     to im T (Thm 3.25)
   - Rank-Nullity as a corollary, not the headline (Cor 3.26)
   - The theorem is algebraic here; it becomes geometric (orthogonal) in Chapter 6

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (core; expected without hesitation):
- [W03.S01] Verify or refute linearity of a described map
- [W03.S02] Compute the kernel and image of a matrix or of a simple operator on polynomials
- [W03.S03] Apply Rank-Nullity to deduce one dimension from the other two
- [W03.S04] Decide injectivity/surjectivity from rank and nullity alone
- [W03.S05] Identify which of the four spaces a given vector or subspace belongs to
- [W03.S06] Describe a quotient space and give its dimension
- [W03.S07] State the Fundamental Theorem correctly, including which space each summand lives in
- [W03.S08] Recognize an isomorphism and produce its inverse on a small example

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Placing the cokernel in the domain, or the coimage in the codomain
- Reporting the kernel as a dimension when a subspace is asked for (or the reverse)
- Assuming rank(A) = rank(A^T) is obvious rather than a consequence of the theorem
- Treating a map with T(0) = 0 as automatically linear
- Confusing the quotient V/U with a complement of U (a complement is a choice; the quotient is not)
- Applying Rank-Nullity with the codomain dimension in place of the domain dimension
