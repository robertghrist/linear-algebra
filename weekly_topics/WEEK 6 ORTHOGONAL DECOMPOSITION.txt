CORE CONCEPTS:

1. Orthogonal Subspaces & Complements (6.1)
   - Orthogonal complement U^perp and its properties (Def 6.1, Lemma 6.2)
   - dim U + dim U^perp = dim V; (U^perp)^perp = U
   - The four fundamental subspaces of a matrix as two orthogonal pairs (Ex 6.3)

2. Projections & Quotients (6.2)
   - Orthogonal projection onto a subspace (Def 6.4)
   - Best Approximation: the projection is the unique nearest point (Lemma 6.5)
   - Projection properties: P^2 = P, P^T = P, im P = U, ker P = U^perp (Lemma 6.7)
   - Matrix forms: P = Q Q^T for an orthonormal basis; P = A(A^T A)^{-1} A^T for independent columns
   - Data centering as a projection onto 1^perp (Ex 6.8)
   - Projection realizes the quotient concretely: the quotient's abstract coset acquires a
     canonical representative

3. The Fundamental Theorem Redux (6.3)
   - Geometric form: coimage = (ker T)^perp and cokernel = (im T)^perp (Thm 6.9)
   - T restricts to an ISOMORPHISM from (ker T)^perp onto im T
   - Rank-Nullity Redux (Cor 6.11)
   - Matrix transformations read through the geometric theorem (Ex 6.10)

4. The Pseudoinverse (6.4)  [MAJOR NEW EMPHASIS IN 2E]
   - Construction from Thm 6.9: invert the isomorphism (ker T)^perp -> im T and extend by
     zero on the cokernel. This sentence IS the definition; the axioms merely make it unique
   - The four Moore-Penrose conditions define it and pin it down uniquely (Def 6.12):
     T T^+ T = T, T^+ T T^+ = T^+, (T T^+)* = T T^+, (T^+ T)* = T^+ T
   - Pseudoinverse Properties (Thm 6.16): T^+ maps im T isomorphically onto (ker T)^perp;
     ker T^+ = (im T)^perp; T T^+ is the orthogonal projection onto im T; T^+ T is the
     orthogonal projection onto (ker T)^perp = the coimage
   - A rank-deficient worked example (Ex 6.13)
   - Redundant manipulators: the minimum-norm solution as the physically chosen one (Ex 6.14)
   - The pseudoinverse of a projection (Ex 6.15)
   - Special cases: A^+ = A^{-1} when invertible; A^+ = (A^T A)^{-1} A^T for independent columns;
     A^+ = A^T (A A^T)^{-1} for independent rows
   - Forward link: the SVD computes it, A^+ = V Sigma^+ U^T (Week 10)

5. Least Squares Approximation (6.5)
   - Normal equations A^T A x = A^T b
   - Existence always; uniqueness exactly when the columns are independent
   - The pseudoinverse solution x = A^+ b is the minimum-norm least squares solution (Thm 6.17)
   - Geometric statement: the residual is orthogonal to the column space
   - Linear regression (Ex 6.18); polynomial overfitting (Ex 6.19)
   - QR (Week 5) is the numerically sound route; the normal equations square the condition number

6. Regularized Least Squares (6.6)
   - Ridge regression: minimize ||Ax - b||^2 + lambda ||x||^2
   - Modified normal equations (A^T A + lambda I) x = A^T b (Lemma 6.20)
   - The regularized problem has a unique solution for every lambda > 0, even when A is rank deficient
   - Effect of lambda: bias against variance; behavior as lambda -> 0 and lambda -> infinity
   - As lambda -> 0+ the ridge solution tends to the pseudoinverse solution

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (core; expected without hesitation):
- Compute an orthogonal complement and verify a dimension count
- Project a vector onto a line, a plane, or the column space of a small matrix
- Recognize a projection matrix from P^2 = P and P^T = P, and identify its image and kernel
- State which fundamental subspace is the orthogonal complement of which
- Write down A^+ for a diagonal, a rank-one, a projection, and an invertible matrix
- Say what A^+ A and A A^+ are, and why they are generally not the identity
- Set up and solve small normal equations; identify the residual and check its orthogonality
- Identify the minimum-norm solution among the infinitely many least squares solutions
- Write the ridge system and describe the effect of lambda

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Asserting A^+ A = I or A A^+ = I in general (both are projections, not identities)
- Using (A^T A)^{-1} A^T when the columns of A are dependent
- Reporting any least squares solution when the MINIMUM-NORM one is requested
- Projecting onto the row space when the column space is meant
- Forgetting to center data before a projection argument
- Claiming ridge regression shrinks the residual (it shrinks the solution)
- Treating the normal equations as numerically equivalent to QR
