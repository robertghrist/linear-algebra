WEEK 4: BASES & COORDINATES
LAEF 2E -- Chapter 4: Bases & Coordinates (Sections 4.1-4.5)
Application sections: Robotic Arm Kinematics; Computer Graphics & Coordinate Systems

CORE CONCEPTS:

1. Bases & Spanning Sets (4.1)
   - Basis as a set that is both spanning and independent (Def 4.1)
   - Basis Extension: any independent set extends to a basis (Thm 4.3, Cor 4.5)
   - Bases for polynomial spaces (Ex 4.2) and matrix spaces (Ex 4.4)
   - Dimension counting as a proof technique (Ex 4.6)

2. Coordinates & Components (4.2)
   - The coordinate vector [v]_B and its uniqueness
   - Coordinates as the isomorphism V -> R^n induced by a choice of basis
   - Components in a non-standard basis (Ex 4.7, Ex 4.8)
   - A vector is not its coordinates; the coordinates depend on the basis

3. Change of Basis (4.3)
   - The change of basis matrix P, written P_{B <- B'} in full (Def 4.9)
   - HOUSE CONVENTION: the matrix is always P (never X); Q is reserved for the orthogonal case;
     V is used only where the columns are literally eigenvectors
   - Direction rule: P_{B <- B'} takes B'-coordinates to B-coordinates
   - The columns of P_{B <- B'} are the B-coordinates of the B'-basis vectors
   - Inverse: (P_{B <- B'})^{-1} = P_{B' <- B}
   - Signal processing (Ex 4.10) and principal stress (Ex 4.11) as motivating changes of frame

4. Matrix Representations (4.4)
   - The matrix [T]_{B',B} of a linear transformation relative to chosen bases
   - Dependence on the basis choice; the standard matrix as one case among many
   - Rotation in different bases (Ex 4.12); projection onto a line (Ex 4.13)
   - Composition of maps corresponds to a product of representing matrices

5. Similarity (4.14) and Coordinate-Free Thought (4.5)
   - B = P^{-1} A P (Def 4.14): the same operator seen in two frames
   - Similarity invariants: rank, nullity, trace, determinant, characteristic polynomial,
     eigenvalues -- and NOT entries, columns, or orthogonality
   - Geometric reading of similarity (Ex 4.15)
   - Which statements about an operator are coordinate-free and which are artifacts of a basis

ESSENTIAL SKILLS (v2, 2026-09-10 -- conceptual: recognize, decide, distinguish, explain why):
- [W04.S01] Compute the coordinate vector of a vector, polynomial, or matrix in a stated basis,
  and know that a vector is NOT its coordinates
- [W04.S02] Know what the change-of-basis matrix P_{B←B′} is (its columns), which direction it
  acts, and that its inverse acts the other way
- [W04.S03] Recognize the matrix of a linear map relative to given bases (columns are images of
  basis vectors, in codomain coordinates) and read kernel/image facts off it
- [W04.S04] Recognize similarity B = P⁻¹AP as the same operator in two frames, and know which
  direction P goes
- [W04.S05] Decide whether a stated property is coordinate-free (rank, nullity, trace,
  determinant, eigenvalues, characteristic polynomial) or a basis artifact (entries, columns,
  orthogonality, singular values)
- [W04.S06] Know that the coordinate map is an isomorphism V → Rⁿ, so linear relations among
  vectors can be read off their coordinate vectors — in any basis
- [W04.S07] Extend an independent set to a basis and trim a spanning set to one; use dimension
  counting as a proof technique
- [W04.S08] Recognize which questions require a basis and which do not (dimension and quotient
  do not; coordinates and matrices do)

TYPICAL MCQ TRAPS:
- Inverting the direction of the change of basis matrix (the single most common error)
- Building P from the coordinates of the OLD basis in the NEW one when the reverse is wanted
- Using P A P^{-1} where P^{-1} A P is required
- Assuming similar matrices have the same eigenvectors (they have corresponding ones, related by P)
- Assuming similar matrices have the same singular values (they need not)
- Confusing [T]_{B',B} with [T]_{B,B'} when domain and codomain bases differ
- Treating orthogonality of columns as similarity-invariant
